# Metrics

Three layers, agreed with Fahad on 2026-10-20. Every number is computed from the workflow or reported by a named person; nothing is estimated. There are no per-consultant scores or rankings, and nothing is labelled "compliance".

Rules: the Metrics section of `apps/web/drizzle/0000_baseline.sql`. Tests: `apps/web/tests/db/metrics.test.ts`.

## 1. Implementation metrics (per project)

`v_project_metrics` is a view with `security_invoker`, so every viewer sees only the projects and rows their row-level security allows. Draft actions are left out. "Today" is the firm's local date (Asia/Riyadh).

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| `actions_total` | Released actions (not draft). |
| `verified` | Actions in Verified Complete. |
| `awaiting_review` | Actions waiting for the firm's review. |
| `blocked` | Actions the owner marked blocked. |
| `overdue` | Open actions (not verified, not closed) whose current due date has passed. |
| `on_time_rate` | % of verified actions verified (date of the last accept review) on or before their **baseline** due date, out of verified actions that have one. |
| `median_days_awaiting_review` | Median days the actions now waiting for review have been waiting. |
| `deadline_slips` | Approved deadline change requests. |
| `revision_rate` | % of reviews that asked for a revision. |
| `stale_actions` | Open actions with no change, progress update or evidence in 14 days. Any change to the action counts, including reassigning its owner. |

`implemented_pct` (see [data-model.md](data-model.md)) completes the set: verified ÷ released actions that still apply.

**Baseline due date.** `actions.baseline_due_date` is set from the due date when the action is released, and a trigger keeps it from changing afterwards. An approved deadline change moves `due_date` (what reminders use) but not the baseline, so on-time is measured against what was agreed, and each move counts as a slip.

**Slip history and aging (F17).** Every move of a released action's due date is kept in `action_due_changes`, whether a request or a plan edit moved it. `app_action_slips()` gives each action's extensions (moves later), the days slipped past the baseline, and, while it is open, how long it is past the baseline, in the buckets 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and over 90 days. `app_project_progress()` adds the counts (`extended`, `extended_twice`, `slip_days`, `late_d1_30`…`late_d90_plus`), so a frozen report keeps them; reports frozen before F17 don't have them and the report page leaves them out. The third move later needs the client's lead consultant or a firm admin.

**Engagement health (F18).** The oversight page gives each planned, active or closing project one signal from counts, never a weighted score (`app_health_signal()`, `src/lib/health.ts`). Off track: a quarter or more of the open actions are overdue, or an open action is more than 60 days past its original date. Watch: anything overdue, or a caution (blocked, moved twice or more, no active owner, stale, a review waiting more than 7 days, a disputed recommendation). On track otherwise; no work yet when nothing is released. The counts come from `app_project_progress()` and the deviations view, so they match the project's own tabs. The daily job (`npm run job:health`, as the schema owner) keeps each project's signal for the current week in `project_health_weeks`; the oversight page shows the last eight weeks beside the signal. The job counts as the owner, so a move of a date is counted even though no user is set (`app_action_extensions()`).

**Monthly status pack (F19).** On the 1st the job (`npm run job:status-packs`, as the schema owner) drafts last month's pack on every active client project and tells the client's lead. The numbers are the report's usual progress numbers at the period's end (`app_project_progress()`); the lists come from `app_status_pack()`: overdue (open, due by the period's end), moved twice or more (open, two or more later moves, `app_action_extensions()`), risk accepted in the period, verified in the period (the day the action reached verified), how many actions reached each status in the period, and what needs a decision (recommendations awaiting the client's answer, pending deadline changes). Only work the client can be shown is listed. The firm adds a note and issues it, which freezes both; then it can be shared.

**History.** `npm run job:metrics` (monthly, as the schema owner) copies each active project's metrics into `metric_snapshots`, one row per metric per day. The table is append-only and read under the same project rules.

API: `GET /api/projects/:id/metrics` returns `{ live, history }`. Readers: the firm, and the client's admins and managers.

Screen: the project's **Progress** tab shows the firm these numbers, the verified share at each monthly snapshot (with a table view), the handover card and the project's reports. A client admin or manager reads the plain part only (page audit U11, batch 1; `src/lib/progress-parts.ts`): the verified share, how the actions ended (verified, partly done, risk accepted, no longer applies, replaced, still open) and the reports the firm shared, with one line saying so; the working measures (median wait, on-time rate, date moves, revisions, what verification rests on, late against the original date, the monthly trend) and the handover card are not drawn for them. Action owners get no tab. Wherever an action owner does read a count (the Projects card, their home page, `app_project_progress()` under their rights), it counts their own actions only: the whole project's numbers are about other people's work (decision 1b; row-level security since migration `0081_owner_actions`).

**Reports.** A report freezes `app_project_progress` as of its period's last day, with optional notes. It starts firm-only and the firm shares it with the client in one step that can't be undone; a correction is a new report.

- API: `GET`/`POST /api/projects/:id/reports`, `GET /api/reports/:id`, `POST /api/reports/:id/share`.
- Clients see only shared reports, and an unshared one reads as not found.
- The report page is `/reports/:id`.

## 2. The team (firm admin only)

- `app_team_workload()`: for each active colleague, sorted by name, the number of clients they are assigned to, the open projects they are staffed on, and the actions waiting for review on their projects. It is for staffing decisions, and carries no score.
- `app_team_metrics(since)`: the team as a whole. Reviews done since the date, the median days from the first evidence of a round to its review, actions waiting for review, and overdue actions.

API: `GET /api/team/metrics` (last 90 days).

Screen: the Team page shows both to the firm admin, under the team list. The workload table is sorted by name.

## 3. Client business indicators (KPI layer B)

The client's own measures of its business (monthly sales, complaints, days to close the books), entered over time beside the work. They are **client-reported, not verified impact**, and every screen and report says so in both languages. On screen the line speaks to its reader: the firm reads "These figures are reported by the client and are not verified impact…", the client's own people "Your company's reported figures, not verified impact…" / «أرقام شركتكم كما أُبلغ عنها، وليست أثرًا موثّقًا…» (the indicator page, the indicators card on the home and a department's page, a recommendation's linked indicators, `/indicators/data`, the import, a shared status pack; `indicators.claimNoteClient`, `indicatorData.claimNoteClient`). The PDF reports and exports keep the third-person line. Migration `0062_client_indicators` replaced the earlier project-level KPI model (objectives, KPIs with green and amber bands, readings), which never had screens.

- `client_kpis`: one indicator of a client, company-wide or for one of its departments (departments stay optional). Name in Arabic and/or English, how it is measured, unit, direction (higher or lower is better), monthly or quarterly, a baseline value and month, an optional target with its date, the source. Once it has a value its baseline, frequency and direction are fixed (start a new indicator to change them), and it is switched off rather than deleted.
- `client_kpi_values`: one value per period on the indicator's grid after the baseline, for a period that has started, with a note. The database records who entered it and when. A change is a correction: a new value that replaces the current one (with a note saying why), and the old value stays in the history. Firm staff may enter values for the client; those are marked "entered by the firm" and the client's admins are told (`indicator.entered_for_you`). Firm staff may also mark a value checked against the client's own source, once, never their own value. That mark is about the figure, not about impact.
- `client_kpi_links`: an indicator and a recommendation of the same client that is expected to move it. It is a stated intent, never a claim of cause. A link to a firm-private recommendation is seen by the firm only.
- `app_kpi_series(kpi, as_of)`: every period from the baseline to the date, with its current value and status: **reported**, **due** (no value yet, within `kpi_grace_days` after the period ends), or **missing** (never zero). Values entered after `as_of` don't count, so a report frozen on a day keeps what was known then.
- `app_remind_kpis(today)`: run by the monthly status-pack job. A client whose last ended period has no value on an active indicator gets one `indicator.due` notice to its admins, at most once in 20 days.
- **An indicator that moved away** (decision 4b, 17 Nov 2026; migration `0090_indicator_moved_away`). A period is judged once, when it has ended, on its latest value: when that value is on the other side of the baseline from the direction the client chose (above it when lower is better, below it when higher is better; equal is not), or the target date has passed and it has not reached the target, the client's lead and deputy and the client's admins get one `indicator.moved_away` notice. A value for a period that has already ended (by any path: typed, a file, a sheet, the client's system) is judged when it is saved; a value for the running period waits until the period ends, and the monthly job judges it then (`app_judge_kpi_periods(today)`, after `app_remind_kpis`). See [below](#when-an-indicator-moves-away).

Who: firm staff on the client and the client's admins set indicators up; **only firm staff link an indicator to a recommendation, or unlink it** (decision 1c, 17 Nov 2026: the link is the consultant's statement that the recommendation is expected to move the number; `indicator.link`, and the writer policies on `client_kpi_links` since migration `0083_indicator_links_firm`), and the client's people read the links they may see; the client's admins and managers, and firm staff, enter values; managers see the company's indicators and their own departments'; action owners and other clients see none (`indicator.*` in `src/authz/policy.ts`, RLS on the three tables).

Which firm staff (decision D4): the firm admin, and a consultant who is the client's lead or deputy, or is staffed (not as an observer) on at least one of the client's projects that is not closed. Every other consultant who works with the client reads the indicators and changes nothing: an observer, someone on the client's team who is staffed on none of its projects, or someone staffed only on closed ones (a closed project is read-only; migration `0075_closed_project_indicators`). Indicators belong to the client, not to a project, so the project observer rule could not cover them; `app_firm_changes_kpis()` (migration `0073_observer_indicators`) and the `working` scope in `policy.ts` do. The buttons follow the same answer on the indicator page, the indicators card, the recommendation page's links and the data page.

Where they show: the client's overview (firm client page and the client admin's or manager's home) beside implementation progress, each department's page, the indicator's own page (`/indicators/:id`: trend line with the target, values with who entered them, corrections, linked recommendations), a recommendation's "Linked indicators" card, the monthly status pack (frozen when issued), the closure report (as of the closing day) and the client report PDF. The assistant answers questions about indicators from these records, under the asker's own rights.

Roll-ups count indicators (how many are up to date, better or worse than their baseline); they never average values or percentages across indicators.

API: `GET/POST /api/clients/:id/indicators`, `GET/PATCH/DELETE /api/indicators/:id`, `POST /api/indicators/:id/values`, `POST /api/indicators/:id/links`, `DELETE /api/indicators/:id/links/:recommendationId`, `POST /api/indicator-values/:id/check`.

How figures arrive is the client's choice, in three modes (typed in, files, connected); see [indicator-data.md](indicator-data.md). Each value records whether it was typed, came from a file, a shared sheet or the client's own system.

Not built, by design: per-vendor connectors to client systems (a client's system sends figures with a key instead), reading arbitrary spreadsheets for analysis, weighting formulas, enterprise-wide dashboards, automatic impact claims, consultant rankings.

### When an indicator moves away

The firm's question was "how do we tell leadership when an indicator moves away from its target?". With figures now arriving by file, sheet or the client's own system, nobody may look at them, so the database tells the people who answer for the indicator.

- **What it says.** Facts only, the figures as the indicator page shows them, and no word that judges: "الشكاوى: ٤٢ شكوى في أكتوبر ٢٠٢٦، خط الأساس ٣٠ شكوى، الأقل أفضل. أرقام يبلّغ عنها العميل، وليست أثرًا موثّقًا." / "Complaints: 42 complaints in Oct 2026, baseline 30 complaints, lower is better. Figures reported by the client, not verified impact." That closing line is for firm readers; the client's own people are spoken to: "أرقام شركتكم كما أُبلغ عنها، وليست أثرًا موثّقًا." / "Your company's reported figures, not verified impact." When the target date had passed on the day the period was judged (the notice's date), the target and its date are named too ("…، والمستهدف ٢٠ بحلول …" / "…, target 20 by …"). Never "worse", "off track", "at risk", a colour or a forecast (`tests/indicator-notice.test.ts` checks both languages). The notice is about the value the period was judged on (`entity_type = 'kpi_value'`); its line states that period's current figure, and it opens the indicator's page.
- **When.** Only a period that has ended is judged, so a month-to-date figure is never compared with a whole month's baseline (`notify_kpi_moved_away()`). A value saved for an ended period (a late figure, an import of past months, a correction) is judged at once when that period is the latest ended one with a value; a value for the running period waits, and the monthly job judges each active indicator's latest ended period it has not told about yet (`app_judge_kpi_periods()`), on the period's latest value then. The target date is looked at only when a period is judged: if no value arrives after the target date, nobody is told about the target; the existing "value due" reminder (`indicator.due`, above) covers missing values.
- **Who is told** (`app_kpi_moved_away_audience()`): the client's lead and deputy consultants while they are active at the firm, and the client's active admins. All of them read every indicator of the client, so no one is told about an indicator they cannot open. Managers are not told (so a manager limited to a department never hears of another department's indicator), nor action owners, other consultants, the firm admin unless they lead the client, or anyone at another client. The person who typed the figure or brought the file in has just seen it and is not told; a sheet read or a system push tells everyone, since no one looked.
- **Once per indicator and period** (`kpi_moved_away_log`, written only by the database). A correction in the same period tells no one again, whichever side it lands on. The line states the period's current figure, so after a correction that stays on the wrong side it shows the corrected one. A notice whose figure a correction has brought back to the right side no longer states a true figure: it is hidden from the people told and never mailed (`app_kpi_notice_fixed()` and the `fixed` policy on notifications); it is kept, not deleted. A period filled in or corrected behind a later ended one says nothing about where the indicator is now and tells no one; a file that fills in several months tells about the latest ended one only.
- **Mail.** The notice goes into the summary mail with the person's reminders (their reminders cadence: daily by default, weekly, or off; `app_queue_digests()`), like a quiet client or a drafted status pack; like all work mail it is a line and a link. Each person told can mute one indicator (**Mute mail** on the indicator's page, beside the action mutes in `mail_kpi_mutes`); the notice still appears in Selah, it is only kept out of the mail. The muted indicators are listed with the muted actions on the notifications page. Someone who is no longer among the people told when the summary runs gets no mail about it, and nobody is mailed a notice whose figure a correction has brought back.
- Not built, by design: thresholds, amber bands, weighting, forecasts, "at risk" predictions, or any link to a recommendation's status.

Rules: `notify_kpi_moved_away()`, `app_judge_kpi_periods()`, `app_kpi_judge()`, `app_kpi_moved()`, `app_kpi_wrong_side()`, `app_kpi_moved_away_audience()`, `app_kpi_tells_me()` and the `mail_kpi_mutes` policy (migration 0090). Tests: `tests/db/indicator-moved-away.test.ts`.

## Verified on whose evidence

A verification is only as strong as what it rests on, so verified actions are counted by basis, never mixed silently (baseline: Insight):

- `app_verified_basis(action)`: `client_evidence` (everything the accepting review covered came from the client in Selah), `consultant_recorded` (all of it was recorded by a consultant for a consultant-managed client), `mixed`, or `internal`. The review covers the submissions it names, or else those of its round; with no evidence at all, the round's progress updates. Null until verified.
- `v_project_metrics` adds `verified_client_evidence`, `verified_consultant_recorded` and `verified_mixed`, and monthly snapshots keep them.
- The action detail returns `verifiedBasis` for the badge.

## The trace and "since your last visit"

- `GET /api/projects/:id/trace` returns the project as edges (`nodeType`, `nodeId`, `parentType`, `parentId`, `label`, `state`): assessment ← finding (a confirmed gap, docs/assessments.md), finding ← recommendation ← action ← evidence or review, indicator ← recommendation (linked as meant to move it), and action ← action (the node supersedes its parent). It runs with the caller's rights, so a firm-private finding or another department's action, and every link to it, are simply absent. Plain Postgres, no graph database.
- `GET /api/projects/:id/since-last-visit` lists the actions that moved (status or plan, progress, evidence, review) since the person last opened the project, using the same function as a meeting agenda's "changed since the last meeting". The first visit looks back 7 days. `PUT` on the same path marks the project as seen. No AI.

## The home page, one per role

`/` answers "what waits for me" first, then "how is it going" (page audit 4.2, decision D2: the Today page became the home, and `/today` and `/group` redirect to it for good). It stores nothing: `home()` in `src/services/home.ts` picks the reader's home by role and reuses the existing readers (`today()`, `listActions()`, `listIndicators()`, `clientOverview()`, `groupRollup()`, `deliverablesToAnswer()`, `listSupportRequests()`, `listInquiries()`, `exceptions()`), so every row is read under the person's row-level security and checked with `authorize()` the way its own page checks it. A section shows five rows and "Show all", which opens the same list in full on the same page (`?all=<section>`), so a count never disagrees with the list it opens; an empty section is not drawn. Each row carries its next step as an outline link ("Answer", "Send a file", "Review", "Decide"); no row has a solid button.

**Firm admin and consultants.** Whose work counts: a consultant's projects are the ones they are staffed on, plus every project of a client they lead or deputise on (as `authorize()` already allows). The firm admin may see everything, so for them it is the projects they are staffed on or lead, and the clients and projects they watch; the page says so. Sections, in this order:

1. **Waiting on your decision**: pending join requests they may decide (the admin, or the client's lead or deputy); pending deadline changes on the projects they lead, not their own; clients' closure requests (the firm admin); request-list items the client answered (`received`) on their projects; a client's comment on a handed-over deliverable (the lead and deputy); recommendations the client disagrees with on their client projects (the "disputed" deviation rule: `app_recommendation_stances()`), for staff who may answer a response; and projects whose closing steps are all done (`app_closure_blockers()` returns nothing), waiting for the final close by whoever may close them (`app_can_close_project()`: the firm admin, or an internal project's lead).
2. **Messages from clients to the firm's management** (the firm admin only): the open ones, each with "Mark handled", and the handled ones folded under them. They used to sit under the notifications.
3. **New inquiries**: inquiries still `new` that the reader may open (every one for the admin, the ones handed to a consultant), with their number beside the title.
4. **Waiting for your review**: actions in Awaiting Review on those projects that the person may verify. Work they own or submitted in the current round is left out (the self-review rule, `canTransition()`), and so is work whose first of two sign-offs they gave (the second is a colleague's, F22), so the list never holds a review the person cannot give.
5. **Late or due this week**: open actions overdue or due within 7 days on the clients and projects the person leads or is staffed on, and their own actions; not on closed projects, and not the ones already in the review list.
6. **Your follow-ups**: open follow-ups assigned to them, overdue first. Each links to its meeting, else its action, else the project.
7. **Your meetings this week**: not cancelled, starting from today to 7 days ahead (firm days, Asia/Riyadh), that they organise or attend, marked when no agenda is written.
8. **Since your last visit**: per open project, how many actions moved since they last opened it, with the same function and 7-day first-visit rule as the project page.

Then **one line of counts** over every action the reader may open (decision D5: counts, never a health word), each a link to the Actions list filtered the same way (the addresses are in `ACTIONS_FILTERS`, `src/lib/home.ts`): an action counts while it is open and its project is not closed; **late** is a due date before the firm's today, **awaiting review** and **blocked** are the action's status, and **reviews waiting over 7 days** have been in Awaiting Review for more than 7 whole days (`firmCounters()`; `tests/db/home.test.ts` holds each counter to the Actions list's own reader with the same rule).

**Client admin and client manager.** What waits for them, in order: recommendations waiting for the company's answer where they are the one who answers (`app_can_respond()` and no answer yet: the admin, or the project's sponsor); the files the firm asked of them by name and not yet sent; their own actions sent back for a revised file; their own actions that are late or stopped; their date requests the firm answered in the last 7 days; meetings they are invited to that wait for their reply, and their next meeting; their open follow-ups; indicators whose latest period waits for a value, when they may enter values; deliverables waiting for their confirmation of receipt; and, for the client admin, the assistant offer and how the company's figures reach Selah, until answered (one row each). Then the progress table per project and per department (`clientOverview()`, as before), the business indicators card (shown when there are indicators, or to an admin while a project still runs; its "New indicator" is an outline button there) with the client report PDF on the progress card, and, for the people the firm named, the group's roll-up (it was `/group`).

**Action owner.** Their own actions in three groups: waiting for them (sent back and late first, then in progress and stopped, then not started; a date they asked for and nobody has answered shows beside the due date), with their consultant, and finished (folded); then the files asked of them, their meetings (to answer, and the next one) and their follow-ups. Nothing else: an owner reads only their own actions (decision 1b), a draft is nobody's at the client (D6), and a department-limited manager's lists hold nothing from another department (D8): all of it is row-level security, which the home reads under.

Not on the page, by design: time tracking, and team load or backlog columns (a later wave).
