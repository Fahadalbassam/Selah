# Meetings and the schedule

Tables in `src/db/schema`, rules in the Meetings section of `drizzle/0000_baseline.sql`. Selah records meetings and links to the call. It never hosts a call. Invites are standard `.ics` files (RFC 5545), which work in Google, Outlook and Apple calendars without connecting any account.

## Booking

`POST /api/projects/:id/meetings` books a meeting. The firm's people on the project can book any meeting. A client admin or manager can book shared ones only. The organiser is added as an attendee.

The same request can carry the attendees and `sendInvites: true`, so one step books the meeting, invites the people and queues the calendar invite. If any attendee is refused, nothing is booked. `app_meeting_invitees(project)` lists whom the caller can invite: for the firm, everyone with an account who can open the project; for a client, its own people and the firm staff on the project, never the firm's whole staff. Only the firm also sees the client's contacts. For each person it also says whether they could open a meeting of the whole project, and their departments (decision 1a, below).

- **The whole project or a department.** At a client with departments, the form asks which: the whole project, or one department (the firm and the client's admin may book for any department, a manager for her own). A meeting of the whole project is booked only by someone who may open it (below): a manager with department limits gets only her departments in the list, and the server and the database refuse the rest (`bookMeeting()`, policy `whole_project` on `meetings`).
- **The invite warning.** When a whole-project meeting would invite someone who could not open it (a manager limited to her departments), the form says so before saving, names the person, and offers to make it the meeting of their department (one button per department the organiser may book for). On a meeting already booked, the add-attendee form says the same for the person picked; a meeting keeps its department, so the offer there is to book a department's meeting. Someone in no department is not sent to "their department's meeting": the warning says no department's meeting would include them either, and to have them added to a department first or leave them out (`LeftOutWarning` in `src/components/meeting-left-out.tsx`, drawn in both languages by `tests/meeting-left-out.test.ts`). Saving anyway is allowed: the person is invited, gets the calendar invite (title, time, link and who else is invited, never the agenda) and cannot open the meeting, whose page and calendar file answer Not found for them. The warning says so in plain words: the calendar invitation still reaches them when it is sent, without the agenda. Its Arabic names the people each line is about instead of a pronoun or verb that fits only a man. The server decides; the form only warns (`leftOutOfMeeting()`, `departmentsToOffer()` in `src/lib/meetings.ts`).

- **Who sees it** (feature A, 17 Nov 2026; migrations `0094`, `0095`; docs/authorization.md, "Who sees an item"). Firm staff on a meeting the firm books, and the client's admin on one a client person books, can narrow it to "everyone with access, except…" or "only these people…", in the booking form, the edit form and on the meeting page (closed by default: "Seen by: everyone with access · Customise"). It only takes people away from those who could already open it, and the list to pick from comes from the server. The organiser and everyone invited, required or optional, always see it (re-check S9, migration 0095): to leave someone invited out, take them off the meeting first. Taking someone off withdraws her invitation if it is not sent yet; if it reached her calendar, she is sent a cancellation for her alone (mail "Taken off a meeting", a calendar CANCEL naming only her address, with no title, time, place or link), once, and not for a meeting already held, cancelled or over. Someone left out gets Not found on the meeting and everything under it (attendees, decisions, summary and its sources, notes, follow-ups, transcript, agenda, calendar file), it is on none of their lists, their schedule or their home, no invitation is queued for them (one still queued is marked withdrawn and kept, and the sender checks again just before each meeting mail goes, so one already picked up is not sent either), and inviting them or giving them a follow-up is refused. Everyone else sees "Limited audience" on it. Client managers and action owners get no picker.

Selah doesn't create Meet links (that needs a Google Workspace connection). The booking form links to `meet.google.com/new` so the organiser can make one and paste it.

- **Call links** must be https on Google Meet, Teams (`teams.microsoft.com`, `teams.live.com`) or Zoom (`zoom.us` and its subdomains). This stops a link that looks like it came from the consultant from pointing anywhere else. The provider is worked out from the link. An online meeting needs a link.
- **The agenda** is written by the database at booking, in the meeting's language. It lists the project's overdue, awaiting-review and blocked actions, and what changed since the last meeting that was held (a department's meeting: that department's). Since migration `0072_agenda_readers` it writes a status in the app's own words and a date the way the screens print it (`app_action_status_label()`, `app_agenda_date()`; a test compares both with `messages/*.json` and the browser's format). Agendas stored before that keep their text; the meeting page maps the old status keys and dates when it draws them (`src/lib/agenda.ts`), so no stored title can be corrupted.
- **The agenda follows the meeting** (decision 1a, option C, 17 Nov 2026; migration `0082_whole_project_meetings`). A department's meeting lists only that department's work; a meeting with no department is the whole project's and lists everything, so it opens only for the people who may see everything (below).
- **Who reads the agenda** (decision D8; the lead's ruling on the review, 17 Nov 2026; migration `0085_live_agenda`). The stored text names work by title as it stood at booking, so it goes only to readers who may see everything on the project (the stable rule below: the firm on the project, the client's admin, a client manager in every department or with departments off). Everyone else who opens the meeting, a department's meeting included, gets the same four lists (late, awaiting review, blocked, changed since the last held meeting) built when they open it from the actions they can read now: an action owner her own ("Only your own actions on this meeting's agenda"), a manager the work she can open ("Only the work you can open on this meeting's agenda"), or a line saying none of it is on the agenda. Work refiled to another department after booking is therefore never named to someone who can no longer open it. For a meeting already past, late work is counted as of the meeting's day, not today; statuses are the current ones. The app role cannot read `meetings.agenda_md` at all; `app_meeting_agenda_for(meeting)` returns the stored text or the reader's own lists. The calendar file and the invitation mails never carry the agenda. A column added to `meetings` later needs its own `GRANT SELECT (column) … TO selah_app`; `tests/db/privacy-leaks.test.ts` fails by name until it has one.
- **Visibility.** A meeting is shared or firm-private. A firm-private meeting, with its decisions and summary, is invisible to the client, and its records cannot be made more visible than the meeting itself.
- **A department's meeting** (booked with a department; migration `0070_department_meetings`) follows the department rule at the client: it is read by the department's people, the client's admins, whoever organises it and anyone invited to it, and by the firm on the project. The same goes for everything under it: attendees, decisions, summary, follow-ups, notes, the calendar file and its line on the schedule. A follow-up still reaches the person it is assigned to. Only someone who sees the department's work books one for it (the firm, the client's admin, the department's people).
- **A meeting of the whole project** (no department; migration `0082_whole_project_meetings`) opens only for people who may see everything on the project: firm staff on the project, the client's admin, and a client manager when the client has departments switched off or when she is a member of every active department of the client. This is stable: it changes only when departments or memberships change, never when the firm releases or refiles an action (the lead's ruling, 17 Nov 2026; migration `0084_whole_project_readers`), plus action owners invited to it. An invited owner reads the meeting's record (summary, decisions, the shared notes with them, attendees and their replies), only the follow-ups assigned to her, and only her own actions on its agenda (migration `0086_owner_follow_ups`). For everyone else, invited or not, the meeting answers Not found, and so do its agenda, calendar file, attendees, decisions, summary and its sources, notes, follow-ups (a follow-up still reaches the person it is assigned to) and transcript; it is on none of their meeting lists or their schedule. The same rule books one (a manager who is not in every department books her department's meeting) and drives the invite warning. Selah has no meeting PDF or export.

## Changes and calendars

`PATCH /api/meetings/:id` moves or edits the meeting, marks it held or cancels it.

- Each meeting keeps one calendar id (`ics_uid`) for life.
- `ics_sequence` goes up on every change a calendar shows: the title, the times, the location or link, and cancelling. Changes that calendars don't show leave it alone.
- A held or cancelled meeting can't be moved, and its status is final.
- `GET /api/meetings/:id/ics` downloads the invite. A cancelled meeting downloads as `METHOD:CANCEL` with the same id, so calendars remove it.
- `POST /api/meetings/:id/invites` queues the invite, or the cancellation, for every attendee with an address. The mail sender builds the `.ics` when it sends.

## Attendees

`POST /api/meetings/:id/attendees` adds a person with an account, or a client contact without one. Free-typed addresses are not accepted, so every address is covered by the erasure rules.

- A person must be able to open the project. For a firm-private meeting, they must also be firm staff.
- Contacts belong to the meeting's client, and only the firm adds them, because only the firm sees the contact list.
- `PUT /api/meetings/:id/response` lets an attendee answer for themselves. That is the only change an attendee can make.
- **Names.** Everyone who may open the meeting reads its attendees' names, and nothing else about them (the lead's decision, 18 Nov 2026; `app_meeting_attendee_names()`, migration `0093_meeting_attendee_names`). An action owner invited with the client's admin sees the admin's name although they share no project; the user-row rule is not widened.

## After the meeting

- **Decisions** (`POST /api/meetings/:id/decisions`) are append-only. A correction is a new decision that supersedes one from the same meeting. Follow-ups can point at the decision they follow.
- **The summary** (`PUT /api/meetings/:id/summary`) can be written only once the meeting is held. Whoever saves it confirms it, and it starts firm-private.
- **Follow-ups** (`POST /api/meetings/:id/follow-ups`, `PATCH /api/follow-ups/:id`, baseline: Meeting follow-ups) come out of a meeting and can follow up one of its decisions. They show on the schedule. They stay in the meeting's project and are never more visible than the meeting. The person assigned must be able to open the project, and must be firm staff for a firm-only follow-up. The firm writes and edits them; the assignee, client side included, can only mark their own one done or open it again.
- Summaries and decisions can be translated on demand (docs/translations.md).
- Not built yet: the AI draft of the summary from a transcript. Meet has no Arabic transcripts, so the summary is written by hand by default.

## Transcripts

A transcript is personal data (PDPL), so Selah keeps it only as long as it takes to write the summary. Rules in the Transcripts section of `drizzle/0000_baseline.sql`.

- **Consent first.** A transcript can be imported only when the meeting records that participants were told it may be transcribed (`transcript_consent`, with who noted it and when), and only once the meeting is held. Switching consent off deletes the transcript at once.
- **Firm only.** The firm's people who run the meeting import, read and delete it (`PUT/GET/DELETE /api/meetings/:id/transcript`). Clients never see it, not even that one exists.
- **Formats.** A pasted text or a `.txt` export (Google Meet saves transcripts as a Doc), or WebVTT/SubRip captions from Meet, Teams or Zoom recordings. Captions are turned into "Speaker: words" lines with timings removed (`src/lib/transcripts.ts`). At most 500,000 characters.
- **Never edited**, only replaced. Imports and deletions are logged without the text.
- **Deleted on schedule.** At most 90 days after import, and `firm_settings.transcript_keep_days` (30) after the summary is confirmed, whichever comes first. `npm run job:purge-transcripts` runs daily as the schema owner.

## Screens

- **Book** from the project's Meetings tab: title, type, the whole project or a department, date, time, length, where, the call link, shared or firm-only, attendees, who sees it (for those who may narrow it), and whether to email the invite now. The link is checked against the same allowlist before saving (`src/lib/meetings.ts`), and anyone invited who could not open a whole-project meeting is named before saving (the invite warning, above).
- **The meeting page** (`/meetings/:id`), before the meeting, shows what a person needs to attend: the time, the call link or the place, the agenda (or the one line that stands in for it), and the attendees with their replies. Whoever runs the meeting can edit or move it while it is scheduled and send the updated invite, send invites, mark it held once it has started, cancel it (in the page's **⋯** menu, behind a question) and send the cancellation, and add or remove attendees. An invited person answers there, in their own voice (**سأحضر · ربما · أعتذر**). An observer on the project reads the page and is offered none of these controls, not even the reply to an invitation: the database refuses each of them (migration `0034_observers`).
- **After it is held**, the page shows its record, for both sides: the summary, decisions and follow-ups (with on-demand translation). Whoever runs it writes the summary (saving confirms it, firm-only unless shared), records decisions (a correction replaces an earlier one, which is then shown struck through), and the firm adds follow-ups. The firm also gets the transcript panel. None of these cards is drawn before (page audit U14, `meetingSections()` in `src/lib/meetings.ts`), except a follow-up someone already wrote, which stays listed. The server rules are unchanged: a follow-up can still be added through the API before the meeting.

Not built yet: Meet's REST API import and creating Meet links (both need a Google Workspace connection), and the AI draft of the summary.

## The schedule

`GET /api/schedule?from=&to=` shows meetings, open actions' due dates and open follow-ups in one list, for up to six months at a time.

- It reads `v_schedule`, which runs with the caller's rights. Each person sees only what they can already see. A client never sees a firm-private meeting or a firm check-in, and a department's meeting and a meeting of the whole project follow the rules above. An action owner gets their own actions and the follow-ups assigned to them only (row-level security since migration `0081_owner_actions`, and the service narrows it too), and the meetings they can open.
- `projectId`, `clientId` and `userId` narrow the list. With `userId`, it shows items for that person: meetings they organise or attend, actions they own, and follow-ups assigned to them. Firm admins use these filters to look at one client, project or colleague.
