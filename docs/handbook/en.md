# The Selah handbook

Selah links what a consulting firm delivers to its client (recommendations, policies, structures) to a person who answers for it at the client, to actions, to evidence the consultant reviews, and to follow-up until the work is closed. This handbook is for the firm and its clients, in plain words, and follows the screens as they are today.

## Where Selah fits in a typical engagement

Almost every consulting engagement goes through the same stages. Selah covers some of them fully, records some, and leaves some to the firm's other tools.

| Stage | What usually happens | What Selah does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First contact | An organisation asks about a service, or the firm approaches one | A public inquiry form (/inquiry) and the **Inquiries** inbox with four steps: read it and give it an owner, hold the discovery session, send the proposal, make it a client. A conflict-of-interest check. A pitch to an organisation that is not a client yet (**Start a pitch**) is seen only by the firm |
| 2. Proposal and contract | The firm writes the proposal and both sides sign | Selah records the signed engagement and its deliverables. It does not write or price the proposal |
| 3. Diagnosis | Interviews, documents, an assessment of where things stand | Lists of documents asked of the client, no-login upload links for one item, assessment templates the consultant fills in (scores stay with the firm) |
| 4. Recommendations | The consultants write what they found and what they recommend | Findings and recommendations go into the **Plan** by hand or from Excel or Word, each tied to what it answers. The client answers each recommendation: agree, partly agree or disagree |
| 5. Implementation and follow-up | The client does the work; the firm follows it | The heart of Selah: an action for each step, an owner and a date, evidence the client sends and the consultant reviews, reminders, meetings with an agenda built from the work itself, reports the firm shares |
| 6. Hand-over | The firm hands over its deliverables | **Hand over** a deliverable's files; the client confirms receipt or writes a comment. Receipt never means the work was accepted |
| 7. Closing | What is still open is settled and lessons are written | The closing steps: a decision on every open action, who carries it on at the client, lessons for the firm only, then the final close and the closure report. The project becomes read-only |
| 8. After the project | Light follow-up, or a new phase | A new project for the same client for each phase, the business figures the client reports (business indicators), and archiving a client that leaves, with an export pack |

What stays outside Selah: writing the consulting content itself, pricing and invoicing, the calls themselves (Selah sends the calendar invitation and the call link only), and judging the quality of the client's work or its compliance. See [What Selah will not do](limits/en.md).

## The six status words

Every list shows one of six words for each item; its own page also shows the exact state:

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Not started** | Released, and work has not started (a firm draft is never shown to the client at all) |
| **In progress** | Work has started |
| **Waiting for the firm** / **Waiting for you** (the firm reads "Waiting for the client") | The next step is on that side: a review at the firm, or a file to send from the client |
| **Needs attention** | The work stopped, for a reason its owner gave |
| **Verified** | A consultant reviewed the evidence and accepted it. The only green word |
| **Closed** | Ended without verification, with how it ended, such as "Closed: risk accepted" |

Late is not a state: the date shows in red beside the word. The full table is in [docs/placement.md](../placement.md).
