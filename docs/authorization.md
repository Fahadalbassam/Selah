# Authorization

Selah decides access on the server from **organization + role + project membership + assignment + visibility label**. A role alone never grants access, and hiding a button is never the control.

There are two layers. Both must allow a request.

| Layer | Where | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| `authorize()` | `apps/web/src/authz/authorize.ts` | Everything: the five questions below, per permission, and the sixth (who sees an item) |
| Row-level security | `apps/web/drizzle/0000_baseline.sql` (Security section) and later migrations | Backstop: tenant, project and label isolation, drafts kept from clients (D6), an action owner's own actions only (1b), whole-project meetings for people who see all of the project's work (1a), people left out of a file or meeting (feature A), plus "only firm can review" |

## The five questions

`authorize(principal, permission, resource)` asks, in order, and the first "no" denies:

1. **Organization.** Client users reach only their own organization. Firm staff reach client records only through a project their firm runs, and a consultant only for clients assigned to them.
2. **Role.** Does `POLICY[permission]` grant this role anything? (`src/authz/policy.ts`)
3. **Project membership or client assignment.** The firm admin sees every project the firm runs, and a client admin every project of their organization. A consultant sees only the projects they are staffed on (`project_members`); the lead of a client sees all of that client's projects. Being on a client's team alone opens the client's name and team, not its projects. Everyone else, including consultants on internal projects, must be a project member.
4. **Scope of the grant.** `any`, `assigned` (e.g. action owners and their actions), `own`, `shared` (explicit per-user shares), `owner_org` (who controls a document's labels; its AI flag also needs `document.assistant`: the person who uploaded that version, scope `own`, or the side's admin), `lead` (the client's lead or deputy), `team` (a department's files), `working` (a consultant who does the client's work: its lead or deputy, or staffed on one of its projects other than as an observer), `staffed` (a consultant who is the client's lead or deputy or is staffed on any of its projects, observing or not: who reads its organisation structure), or `engagement` (a consultant who is the client's lead or deputy or is staffed, not as an observer, on one of an engagement's open projects: who hands its deliverables over).
5. **Visibility label.** `shared`, `firm_private` (firm only), `client_private` (that client only, not even the firm admin), `user_private` (creator only).

After the five, and after the rules of state (closed project, archived client, observer), a **sixth question** only ever says no: was the item (a document that is not evidence, or a meeting) narrowed with the picker, and is this person left out of it? (`resource.audience`, loaded from the database; "Who sees an item" below.) A denial answers 404, like the others.

## Internal and client projects

A project has a `kind`. An **internal** project belongs to the firm (`org_id = firm_org_id`, enforced by the `check_project_orgs` trigger); client roles never see it. A **client** project belongs to a client organization. The firm admin manages `client_assignments`, which is what places a consultant over a client. RLS mirrors this in `app_can_see_project()` and `app_is_assigned_to_client()`.

## Staffing (who works on which client)

Decided with the product owner on 2026-10-20:

- Only the **firm admin** adds clients and names one **lead consultant** per client (`client_assignments.is_lead`).
- **A change of lead** (Gate B, item 6.2, migration `0109_lead_change_end_reminder`) is recorded in `client_lead_changes` with an optional **handover note** for the new lead. The note is firm only: the firm admin, the old and the new lead and the client's current lead and deputy read it; the client, other consultants on the team and other firms never do (policy `lead_change_read`). Nobody writes the record through the app role: the firm admin's change goes through `app_change_client_lead()` (refused for a client another firm works on, an archived client, or a new lead who is not an active consultant of the admin's firm). The client's admins read only the new lead's name, through `app_lead_change_notices()`, which answers only an active admin of that client or the old lead, whatever notice rows exist; the old lead is told the client was handed over. Tests: `tests/db/lead-change-end-reminder.test.ts`.
- The **lead** adds or removes other consultants on that client (`client_assignment.manage`, scope `lead`). Only the admin changes or removes the lead.
- Consultants see a **directory of client names only** (`app_client_directory()`, no projects, people or files) and can **ask to join** with a reason (`join_requests`). The admin or that client's lead approves or declines; a decision is final and audited.
- Being on a client's team makes a consultant **eligible**; the client's lead (or the admin) **staffs them on each project**, and they see only those projects (decided 2026-10-20). Leaving the client team takes them off its projects.
- The **firm admin** sees everything. To get notifications they either **co-consult** (staff themselves on a project) or **watch** a client or project (`watches`, `project.watch`). Other admins get no notifications for work they don't follow.
- Work sent for review notifies the project's firm audience: the client's lead, the staff on the project (an internal project's leads), and watching admins (`app_notify_project_team()`).
- A client has **many projects** (one per phase or separately agreed scope, decided 2026-10-24, option B). The firm admin and the client's lead create them from the client page (`POST /api/clients/:id/projects`) with an optional phase number, planned working days, dates and the project it continues; the admin corrects them later (`PATCH /api/projects/:id`).
- **Client people on a project** (`/api/projects/:id/people`): client managers and action owners see only the projects they are on, and an action's owner must be on its project. The firm admin, the client's admin and the client's lead consultant add the client's active people from the project's People tab; nobody comes off while they own open actions there (`check_member_removal()`). A restricted client has only admin accounts; a consultant-managed client has none, so its owners are named on each action instead.
- Any firm member can create an **internal project** and becomes its lead (`project_members.is_lead`).
- On internal projects firm staff do the work themselves (`INTERNAL_POLICY` in `policy.ts`), and **nobody reviews their own action** (`workflow:self-review`, mirrored in the `reviews` insert policy).

- **Client business indicators** belong to the client, not a project (`indicator.*`, migration `0062_client_indicators`). Firm staff who work with the client and the client's admins set them up. **Only firm staff link an indicator to a recommendation, or unlink it** (decision 1c, 17 Nov 2026; `indicator.link`, migration `0083_indicator_links_firm`): the link is the consultant's statement that the recommendation is expected to move the number, so the client's admin no longer makes it (the API answers 404, the database refuses), and every client reader still sees the links they may see. Admins and managers, and firm staff, enter values; a manager sees the company's indicators and their own departments'; action owners see none. A link to a firm-private recommendation is visible only to whoever can read that recommendation (docs/metrics.md).
- **Who is told when an indicator moves away** (decision 4b, migration `0090_indicator_moved_away`): the client's lead and deputy, while active at the firm, and the client's active admins, nobody else (`app_kpi_moved_away_audience()`). All of them read every indicator of the client, so nobody is told about an indicator they cannot open; a manager (in any department), an action owner, another consultant, the firm admin who does not lead the client and anyone at another client are never told. The notice is read back under the reader's own rights, and the summary mail leaves it out for someone no longer among the people told. Each person told may mute mail about one indicator (`mail_kpi_mutes`: their own rows, only for an indicator they can read). Tests: `tests/db/indicator-moved-away.test.ts`.
- **Which firm staff change a client's indicators** (decision D4, 2026-11; migration `0073_observer_indicators`). Because indicators are the client's, the observer rule below (which looks at the project a record is in) never reached them. So the rule is written from the other side: a consultant enters, corrects or checks a value, adds, edits, pauses or deletes an indicator, links or unlinks a recommendation, or changes how the figures reach Selah **only when staffed, not as an observer, on at least one of that client's projects that is not closed, or as its lead or deputy**. Every other consultant who works with the client (an observer, someone on the client's team staffed on nothing, or someone staffed only on closed projects: a closed project is read-only, migration `0075_closed_project_indicators`) reads. A lead or deputy never observes the client's projects: naming one ends their observing. The firm admin and the client's own roles are unchanged. In `authorize()` this is the `working` scope, from `principal.workingClientIds`; in the database, `app_firm_changes_kpis()` behind every write policy on the indicator tables. Tests: `tests/db/observer-indicators.test.ts`.

RLS for all of this is in the Staffing and Staffed visibility sections of `drizzle/0000_baseline.sql`.

The `Principal` is built from the session and the database (`services/principal.ts`), never from anything the browser sends.

An **action owner** opens only their own actions, and is not told how many others there are (decision 1b, "Action owners see only their own actions" below). A **client manager at a client with departments switched on** opens company-wide work and the work filed under their own departments (`app_dept_ok()`); the client's Departments page promises exactly that. Where a list leaves something out, the screen says how many, never what. Tests for every row: `tests/db/privacy-leaks.test.ts`.

## The client's organisation structure

Gate A follow-ups (17 Nov 2026, feature B; migration `0091_org_chart`; the data in [data-model.md](data-model.md), "The client's organisation structure"). The structure belongs to the client, not to a project, like its indicators. **It never grants or limits access to any work**: a unit linked to a department is a label; who sees an action, a file, a meeting or an indicator is decided by departments and the rules above exactly as before, and no policy outside the chart's own tables reads them (`tests/db/org-chart.test.ts` proves that what every client person reads is the same before and after a chart with linked units is published).

| Who | Reads | Names of the people in positions | Changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firm admin | The draft, the current version, the history | All | Edits the draft, publishes, changes holders (and removes one entered by mistake), answers suggestions |
| The client's lead or deputy | The same | All | The same |
| A consultant staffed on one of the client's projects (not observing, project not closed) | The same | All | Edits the draft, changes holders (and removes one entered by mistake), answers suggestions; does not publish |
| An observer, or a consultant staffed only on closed projects | The same | All | Nothing |
| A consultant on the client's team staffed on none of its projects | Nothing (404) | | |
| The client's admin | The current version and the history, never the draft | All | The people in positions on the published structure (and removes one entered by mistake there, never in the draft); the "show names" switch; suggests changes |
| A client manager | Every unit and position title of the current version and the history | Only on the current version, as of today, in her branch and the chain above it, and none while its "show names" is off; never a holding's dates; older versions are titles only | Nothing |
| An action owner | No chart (404). "Your manager" at the head of their own menu (their profile): the position theirs reports to and who holds it (the name only while names are shown) | | Nothing |
| Anyone of another client, or a firm person outside the client | Nothing (404) | | |

- **The branch.** The units linked to the manager's departments and every unit under them, plus the chain above it: the positions those positions report to, up to the top, and the head positions of the units above. A client with departments off gives a manager no department of her own, so no branch and no names (`app_org_chart_branch()`).
- **Names are decided in the database.** A client manager cannot read `position_holders`; `app_org_chart_holders()` sends a name only where `app_org_chart_named_positions()` allows it, and an unnamed holder arrives as filled with no name, person, dates or id. The page never receives a name to hide. The snapshot of a published version carries no names.
- **One switch, today only.** Holdings follow the position across versions, so for a client manager names follow the current version's switch alone: she reads holders only on the current version, as of today whatever day is asked, and never a holding's dates or id; an older version is titles only for her, whatever its own switch said. The firm's readers and the client's admins keep the whole history, on any version and day (`app_org_chart_full_reader()`).
- **"Your manager"** (`app_my_manager()`) is for anyone at the client whose own position is on the current published structure, whatever their role, and whether the client has departments on or off: the position theirs reports to and whether it is vacant, and its holder's name only while the current version shows names (always for the client's admin). It names only their own manager, so it does not follow the manager's branch rule: at a client with departments off, a manager's chart shows no names while her "your manager" does when names are on.
- **"Show names"** is on each published version, owned by the client's admin (`app_set_org_chart_show_names()`), off by default (names are personal data under the PDPL) and carried over to the next published version.
- **Minimal records** (consultant-managed): titles only. No holder can be stored, and switching a client to minimal records is refused while its structure holds anyone's name, now or in the past; nothing is deleted to make way for it (`check_minimal_records()`, shown on the client page's engagement card). **Restricted clients** have admins only, who see everything as above.
- **Structure only in the draft.** Units and positions change only while their version is the draft (`check_org_unit()`, `check_position()`), only by the firm's editors (row-level security, `app_org_chart_editor()`); the client's admin and managers get a 404 from the services and a refusal from the database. Publishing is `app_publish_org_chart()` (the firm admin, the lead or the deputy, `app_org_chart_publisher()`); the app cannot write the chart or its versions otherwise.
- **People in positions.** People are placed only on the live structure, the current published version: by the client's admin and by the firm's editors, never in the firm's draft (`check_position_holder()`, for everyone; the lead's decision B1 in the part 3 review: a draft never shows the company a planned successor, so the draft holds nobody). In the outline editor, who holds a position is read only, with a link to **People in positions**. A change on the published structure is audited and tells the client's lead and deputy (`org_chart.holder_changed`, `notify_position_holder()`, `app_org_chart_tell_lead()`).
- **Removing a holding entered by mistake** (part 3, migration 0097; lead's decision, 18 Nov, data protection). Besides ending a holding, the same people may remove one typed by mistake (a typo, the wrong person): the client's admin and the firm's editors, on positions of the published structure; nobody else. The app has no delete right on `position_holders`; it removes one only through `app_remove_position_holder()`, which checks who asks, deletes the row, writes the audit row (`org_chart.holder_remove`: the position, who and when, never the name) and tells the lead and deputy for a published position. The screen asks first, in plain words, that it cannot be undone. Names still kept on positions a later version dropped (their holdings ended when it was published) are listed under **People in positions** ("Names on positions no longer in the structure", `droppedHoldings()`) and removed the same way, so a client that restructured can still switch to minimal records (S1 of the part 3 review). Once no holding names anyone, the client can be switched to minimal records again.
- **Leaving and joining** (part 3). Switching a client person off ends every holding of theirs that has started on the leaving day, also one that had a later end date, and cancels one that has not started yet (the row removed, audited as a count without names), both listed on the leaving report first (`end_holdings_on_leaving()`; the lead's decision B2 in the part 3 review); whoever switches them off (the client's admin or the firm admin: `org_user.manage`) may name a replacement for each position, who holds it from the next day (`setActive()` through `changeHolder()`, so the same holder rules apply). An invitation may name a vacant position of the current structure, only by someone who may change holders there (`check_invitation_position()`, `vacancyLineage()`); accepting it starts the holding when the position is still on the current structure and has room, checked under the chart's publish lock; otherwise the person who invited is told, so they can place them (`app_accept_invitation()`).
- **A person's work card** (part 3; Fahad, 18 Nov). On the drawing, a holder with a Selah account whose name the reader sees is a button that opens a card with their open tasks, what is late and their recent uploads (`GET /api/clients/:id/structure/people/:userId`, `personWork()`). The card opens only where the reader already sees that name on that version (read through `getStructure()`: the manager's branch rule, "show names" and minimal records all apply); a typed name has no card; an action owner has no tab and no card; anyone else gets a 404. Everything on it is read as the reader, through `listActions()` and `listFiles()` under their own row-level security, so it holds only what they could already open (another department's action, a firm draft, and a file kept from them by its label, its department or its audience ("everyone except…", "only…": feature A) are absent); nothing reads on anyone else's behalf. It shows the work, never a score, count, ranking or colour about the person.
- **Suggest a change.** The client's admin writes one (what, where, why) from the Structure tab ("Suggest a change / اقترح تعديلاً", `POST /api/clients/:id/structure/requests`); it tells the lead and deputy (`org_chart.change_requested`; one unread notice per client at a time and one mail a firm day for each client, as for holder changes) and waits on their home under "Waiting on your decision" (part 5, `openRequestsToDecide()`); the firm's editors mark it done or declined, once, from its row menu on the client's Structure tab (`POST /api/org-chart/requests/:id`), and the admin who wrote it gets a plain notice (`org_chart.request_done` or `org_chart.request_declined`, `notify_org_chart_request_answered()`). The client's admin reads every suggestion of the company and its answer; managers and action owners see none.
- **Publishing (part 5).** `/clients/:id/structure/publish` and `POST /api/clients/:id/structure/publish` are for the firm admin, the lead and the deputy (`org_chart.publish`); a staffed consultant, an observer, every client person and anyone of another client get a 404. The screen lists what changes, the people on dropped positions and what happens to each planned person; the list of plans comes from `app_org_chart_plan_outcomes()`, which gives the draft's editors their names and anyone else nothing. Publishing places the planned people from the day the version takes effect (each re-checked as still an active person of the client, else skipped and listed), clears the plans, and tells the lead and deputy; the board resolution must be a file of the client that the client can see and the publisher may open; the day is today or later and on or after the current version's (never back-dated).
- **The department-head suggestion (part 5).** Where the person holding a linked unit's head position is not that department's head in Selah, the client's admin and the firm admin (`department.manage`) see a suggestion on the Structure tab (`headSuggestions()`); nobody else does. **Nothing changes on its own**: holding the head position gives nobody access to the department's work. Confirming (`POST /api/clients/:id/structure/head-suggestions`, `confirmHeadSuggestion()`) is the same change as on the Departments page, with the same permission, said in plain words before it is made: the person becomes the department's head (and a member if they were not, so from then on they see its work), and the former head stays a member.
- **"Show names" on screen (part 5).** The client's admin has the switch on the current version, on the Structure tab of the company's People page (`POST /api/org-chart/versions/:id/show-names`); anyone else gets a 404.
- **Where it is read (part 2, read only).** Firm: the **Structure / الهيكل** tab of the client page (`/clients/:id/structure`, beside Overview and Departments), shown only to the people in the first four rows above who read the chart (`canReadStructure()`, `org_chart.read`); it shows the current version, the firm's draft marked "the client does not see it" and earlier versions by number and date. Client: the **Structure / الهيكل التنظيمي** tab of the company's People page (`/departments/structure`, beside People and Departments: page audit 4.1), for the client's admins (a restricted client has only these) and managers; action owners have none of these pages (404). When the company does not use departments, a manager has People and the Structure (the Departments page sends her there); the client's admin keeps the Departments tab too, because the switch that turns departments on is there. No sidebar row: the People row stays marked on these tabs. Both read through `getStructure()`, which calls `getPublishedChart()`, `getDraft()` and `getVersion()` and decides nothing again: a version id outside this client's own list (another client's, the draft for a client person, unknown) is a 404. The PDF (`GET /api/clients/:id/structure/pdf`, `orgChartPdf()`) reads the same and holds the same names; "without names" drops every one; it is audited (`org_chart.export`). **The tabs show only when there is something to read** (`hasStructure()`): for the client, a published version; for the firm, a published version or a draft with at least one unit. Until then there is no tab, and the Structure address answers Not found, so no page offers an action the app does not have yet. ## Draft actions

Decision D6 (product owner, 17 Nov 2026): **a client does not see a draft action until the firm releases it.** This reversed the earlier behaviour, where a client admin or manager could list and open a draft. A draft is an action with status `draft`; the firm releases it (Draft → Assigned, `action.release`). Firm roles see drafts exactly as before.

No client role (client admin, client manager, action owner, even the person the draft names as owner) reads the draft or anything under it: progress, evidence and its evidence list, reviews and sign-offs, risk acceptances, date requests, firm notes, the discussion, due-date history, reminders and follow-ups, mail mutes, closure items, the activity events and notifications about it, translations of its text and its assistant passages. A client can write none of these either. Opened by its address or through the API it answers 404, like a missing record. When the firm deletes a draft (only drafts are deleted), its activity events and any notice about it stay hidden from client people; they are kept for the firm. Lists, counts, "N more" lines, exports, reports, status packs and assistant answers for a client leave drafts out.

## Who sees an item (the picker)

Decision of 17 Nov 2026, feature A ("everyone except…" / "only…"). Restructuring proposals, salary and disciplinary files and closed board sessions are kept need-to-know until they are announced; departments and labels cannot keep one file from one manager in the same department. The picker does that, and **it can only take people away: it never gives anyone access that the role, project, department, label, draft and owner rules do not already give.** Sharing a file with one more person stays the separate one-by-one share.

**The effective rule.** (today's rules OR a one-by-one share) AND NOT left out AND (no "only" list, OR on it). Two modes: **"Everyone with access, except…"** (the default position of the switch; the people named are left out, and anyone who gains access later sees the item) and **"Only these people…"** (nobody else, ever, until it is changed; saving it with nobody is refused). No audience at all is "everyone with access", the normal case. **Left out beats a share**: a share, an invitation to a meeting, making an attendee required and assigning a meeting's follow-up to someone left out are each refused, never a silent lift of the exclusion.

**Locked people.** Some people always see the item, so the work cannot stall. The server works them out live from the item and the client, never from the page and never frozen at save time: the person who set the audience, whoever uploaded any version of the file, the client's admin (on anything the client can see), the firm admin (on the firm's items), the client's lead and deputy, and a meeting's organiser and everyone invited to it, required or optional ("Invited to this meeting"; the product owner's decision on the re-check, S9): to leave someone invited out, take them off the meeting first, which withdraws an invitation not sent yet or sends their calendar a cancellation naming only them; and on a file sent for an information request, the person it asks ("Asked for this file"; re-check N4), so she never sees "Received" with nothing to open: a file she is left out of is not sent for her item, and the item is not given to someone left out of a file sent for it. A lead or deputy named after an "only" list was saved is let in at once; leaving out a locked person is refused. Whoever saves an "only" list is also written onto it as an ordinary pick (the product owner's decision on the review, 18 Nov 2026): locked to themselves while they edit, a normal chip to the next editor, who removes them only on purpose. Nobody is ever written onto a list of people left out.

**Whose item it is, and who uses the picker.**

| Item | Whose | Who sets it | Who can be left out |
|---|---|---|---|
| A deliverable, a firm upload (shared with the client or not), a meeting firm staff booked | the firm's | firm staff who manage it (the file's owning side, a meeting's runners); not observers | firm colleagues and client people, except the locked ones |
| A client upload, a meeting a client person booked | the client's | the client's admin | the client's own people only: a client cannot hide its items from the firm this way (`client_private` does that), so on an "only" list the firm's people still see it |

Client managers and action owners get no picker; the server answers them 404 (`document.audience`, `meeting.audience` in `policy.ts`; `app_can_manage_audience()` in the database).

**Where.** Documents of every kind except evidence (a deliverable, a client document, an information request's file, every version of each), and meetings (with their attendees, decisions, summary and its sources, notes, follow-ups, transcript, calendar file and invitation mail). Not on actions, recommendations, evidence, indicators or the org chart: those are the shared plan.

**People left out see nothing**: no row, no count, no "1 hidden item", no notice about the item or about the change, no invitation, no passage in the assistant. Everyone who still sees a narrowed item gets the "Limited audience" marker on it. Opening it up again ("everyone") tells nobody either.

## How a request flows

```
route()  ─ CSRF origin check, session, Zod validation
  └─ asUser(userId)  ─ one transaction; set_config('selah.user_id') so RLS applies
       └─ loadPrincipal()
            └─ service  ─ load the row (RLS hides other tenants entirely)
```

## Workflow

`src/authz/workflow.ts` lists every allowed status move and the permission it needs:

| Move | Permission | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Draft → Assigned | `action.release` | Firm admin, consultant (until then the client does not see the action: "Draft actions" above) |
| Assigned → In Progress, In Progress ↔ Blocked, In Progress → Awaiting Review, Revision Requested → In Progress | `action.work` | Action owner (own actions), client manager; on a restricted client, the client admin on the actions they own; on a client the firm runs, firm staff on the project |
| Awaiting Review → Verified Complete or Revision Requested | `action.review` | Firm admin, consultant |
| Any open status → Closed (with an outcome) | `action.close` | Firm admin, consultant |
| Verified Complete or Closed → In Progress | `action.reopen` | Firm admin, consultant |

The database trigger `check_action_transition` holds the same list and also requires an `accept` review before Verified Complete. Only firm staff can insert reviews (RLS). So a client claim can never become "verified".

**A restricted client** has admin accounts only, and the firm records only for clients it runs. So on a restricted client its admin does the owner's work on the actions they own, as an action owner does on a full client: start, "I am stuck" and resume, progress notes, evidence, send and resend for review, and asking for more time (`action.work`, `progress_update.create`, `evidence.submit`, `deadline_request.create` granted to `client_admin` as `assigned` in `RESTRICTED_POLICY`, `src/authz/policy.ts`). On an action another admin owns, or nobody owns, the admin gets a 404, and the database refuses it too (`app_restricted_admin_works()`, migration 0107), including a change to the "I am stuck" reason. To work it, the admin first gives it to themselves (decision D1, below: recorded as `action.owner_change`, and the previous owner is told), then works it. A full client's admin keeps the rights in the table above, and does not record progress or evidence (owner decision, 19 Nov 2026).

Nobody reviews or closes an action they own or submitted evidence for. The trigger (Follow-through section of the baseline) also refuses, for client users: releasing, reviewing, closing and reopening, and any change to the plan columns (title, description, due date, closure fields). Who may change the owner is decision D1, below. Writes to findings, recommendations, meeting notes, follow-ups and reports are firm-only in RLS. See [data-model.md](data-model.md) for closure outcomes, evidence lists, deadline requests and reminders.

Nobody is told about a draft. The one who made the change is not told.
- Recorded: one audit row `action.owner_change` with the user ids, or `typed` for a typed name (never the name), and a line in the action's history ("gave it to …"). Everyone who may open the action reads that line, and the new owner reads "given to you by … on …" (review S1, the coordinator's decision): through `app_action_owner_changes(action)` (migration `0102_action_owner_history`), which answers only for one action, only to someone `app_can_see_action()` lets open it, only when, who and to whom, and for a client reader nothing from before the release. The activity record's own rules are not widened.
- One rule decides it everywhere: `app_can_change_owner(action)` (migration `0101_action_owner`), read by the update trigger for both sides (for the firm since `0103_action_owner_firm`, review S5: not on a closed project, not as an observer), by `app_tell_owner_changed()` (refused to anyone else, and since `0104_action_owner_notices` it tells only of the change the caller has just made, as the service recorded it in the same transaction, so it cannot send false notices) and by `ownerControl()` / `changeActionOwner()` in `src/services/action-owner.ts` (`POST /api/actions/:id/owner`). The firm's edit dialog (`updateAction()`) goes through the same rule, notices and record. `authorize()` grants `action.assign_owner` to firm staff, the client admin and client managers; the database narrows managers to the coordinator and sponsor.
- Handing over everything a leaver holds (Team, `app_hand_over()`) stays the firm admin's and the client admin's, as before, in the switch-off dialog. On a project's People tab, "Reassign their actions first" opens that person's open actions on this project, each with its own owner control, for everyone who may reassign them (`reassignList()`; review S3, the coordinator's decision); it never opens the switch-off dialog.

Tests: `tests/db/action-owner.test.ts` (who may and may not, own organisation only, status unchanged, who is told, the audit row, minimal records), `tests/db/follow-through.test.ts`.

### Second sign-off (F22)

Some work is verified only after two consultants have checked it. It applies when the client's rule says so (`client_review_settings`: off, after two revisions, high and critical priority work, or every action; each level includes the ones before), or when the action's own switch is on. The firm admin, or the client's lead or deputy (`organization.second_review`), sets the rule on the client page. The same people, or an internal project's lead (`action.second_review`), turn an action's switch on or off.

On such work the first consultant who accepts records a sign-off (`app_sign_off()`, written to `review_signoffs` with the evidence hashes they saw), and the action stays Awaiting Review. Someone else's accept then verifies it. Neither sign-off can come from the owner or from anyone who submitted evidence in the round, and the second never comes from the first reviewer. The trigger `check_second_review` refuses Verified Complete without a first sign-off from a different consultant, and refuses the switch to anyone but the people above. Either reviewer may ask for a revision instead. The next round then needs both sign-offs again. Who could give the second sign-off is told in Selah. The list is the project's staff and the client's lead and deputy. When none of them is left, it falls back to the firm's admins. Clients see that a second consultant checks the work, never the rule or the names of who could check it.

## AI retrieval

`retrievalScope()` turns the same check into a filter (project, tenant, allowed labels, user). `services/assistant.ts` puts that filter **inside** the pgvector query's `WHERE`, and the query also runs under RLS. Chunks are deleted by trigger when a document's AI flag is turned off, its label changes, or it is archived. The AI service only receives the passages that survived.

Client admins and managers get the assistant only where the firm admin offered it to their role and their admin agreed (wave 3, F39): `assistant.query` lists them, `authorize()` also requires `principal.clientAssistant` (from `app_client_ai_on()`), and RESTRICTIVE policies on `ai_chunks` and `ai_conversations` check the same in the database. Action owners stay excluded because their document access is per-user shares, which the index does not carry. See [assistant.md](assistant.md), "The client assistant".

## File explorer

The Files sheet in the sidebar (`GET /api/files`, `services/files.ts`) lists documents from the database, never from the bucket. Rows come back filtered by RLS (project, label, department, someone else's unsubmitted evidence), then each passes `authorize("document.download")` with its per-user shares. So the explorer shows exactly the files a person could already open elsewhere:

| Who | Sees |
|---|---|
| Firm admin | Every file of every client the firm runs, and internal work. Never a client-private file. |
| Consultant | Files of the projects they are staffed on (all of a client's projects when they lead it). Never client-private files. |
| Client admin | All of the company's files, including client-private ones. Nothing of the firm or other clients. |
| Client manager | The company's files outside departments, and their own department's. |
| Action owner | Their own uploads, their department teammates' files (decided 2026-10-22: the team sees each other's work, the manager and client admin see everything below), and files shared with them one by one. A teammate's evidence file never names the teammate's action (decision 1b). |

Seeing a file is not changing it. Only the person who uploaded a file, or an admin of the side that owns it, may rename it or move it to another department (no screen does either today; `check_document_rename()`, migration `0106`), and nobody changes which file a version replaces (`check_document_lineage()`). A file answering a confidential information request stays out of the assistant in every version (`check_confidential_file()`, migration `0106`).

A file shared with someone one by one reaches them even when it is filed under another department (baseline: Document shares across departments). A file narrowed with the picker reaches nobody it leaves out, shared or not, and shows "Limited audience" to the rest ("Who sees an item" above).

## Observers

An observer on a project (a firm consultant, migration `0034_observers`) reads everything there and changes nothing, except adding a firm note to an action and asking the assistant. `authorize()` refuses every other write on a record of that project, and the trigger `check_not_observer()` refuses it in the database. The screens draw no control the database would refuse: on a meeting the observer gets no edit, invite, cancel, attendee, decision, summary, follow-up or reply control (`canRun`, `canFollowUp`, `canRespond` in `getMeeting()`), and under Sources on the project's Plan no "Start an assessment". On the client's business indicators an observer reads only (see Staffing above).

Files are grouped client, then project, then source (evidence by action, deliverables, client documents, meetings, assessments). Only the latest version is listed; downloads go through the usual one-minute signed link.

**The assistant switch on a file** ("available to the assistant", Gate B, D1, 19 Nov 2026): switching it on is for the person who uploaded that version of the file, or an admin of the side that owns it (the client's admin on the client's files, the firm admin on the firm's). Switching it off stays open to anyone on the owning side (the lead's ruling on the review, S1). Another client manager, or another consultant, sees a file that is off with a line saying who may make it available, and no switch; `POST /api/documents/:id/assistant` with `on: true` answers them 404 (`document.assistant` in `policy.ts`), and the database refuses the change (`check_document_assistant()`, migration `0108_privacy_batch`). Whoever uploads a new version is its uploader. Uploading a new version or withdrawing a file still takes the old row out of the assistant, whoever does it. Tests: `tests/db/privacy-batch.test.ts`.

**Reusing a file as evidence** (`POST /api/actions/:id/evidence/existing`) copies the stored object under a new random key and records a new evidence document. The original keeps its label, department and history; the copy follows the evidence rules. It is refused unless the person can submit evidence on the action and download the original, and the original belongs to the same client, is `shared` (never firm-only or private) and was cleared by the virus scan. Tests: `tests/db/files.test.ts`.

**Adding a file to a project** (`POST /api/projects/:id/documents`, the Files card's "Add a file") is open to firm staff on the project and to client admins and managers. `GET /api/files/targets` lists the projects and the choices each side has: firm staff label a file firm-only or shared (internal work is always firm-only); a client labels it shared or private to the company (a restricted client's files start private); a client admin can file it under any department, a manager under their own. Files are scanned like evidence. The upload is audited but kept off the project's activity feed, which the firm and the client's admin see.

## Adding a new record type (checklist)

1. Add the table in `src/db/schema/app.ts` with `project_id`, `org_id` (via `scoped()` and `scopedFk()`) and, if it can be private, `visibility`.
2. `npm run db:generate`, then add grants and an RLS policy in a new custom migration (`npx drizzle-kit generate --custom`). Without them the app role cannot touch the table. `tests/db/rls.test.ts` fails if RLS is off.
3. Add permissions to `POLICY` with the narrowest grants that work.
4. Write the service: load the row, build the `Resource`, call `assertAllowed()`.
5. Expose it with `route()`.
6. Add allow **and** deny tests: other tenant, other project, wrong role, wrong label.
